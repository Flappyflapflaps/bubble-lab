In conventional, classical systems, the structural architecture of an aqueous foam lamella relies exclusively on the spontaneous, reversible adsorption of soluble surfactants at the air-water interface. These amphiphilic molecules operate by lowering the interfacial tension of the bulk fluid and introducing a degree of Gibbs-Marangoni elasticity, which acts as a self-healing mechanism against localized film stretching. However, these classical surfactant-stabilized systems are inherently metastable and doomed to eventual failure. As time progresses, the macroscopic thin film is subjected to inescapable destabilization mechanisms: pure viscous-gravitational drainage, powerful capillary suction into the surrounding Plateau borders, relentless evaporative thinning into the ambient atmosphere, and the eventual transition into the catastrophic black film regime.
