# Real Raw-Surfactant Giant Bubble Recipes

Primary-source harvest. Verbatim quantities only. Where the source could not be retrieved, marked **UNAVAILABLE**.

> **Important reality check:** the giant-bubble practitioner literature is overwhelmingly built on Dawn / Fairy / Dreft as the surfactant. Every Soap Bubble Wiki "named recipe" except **RAD** uses commercial dish detergent. The raw-surfactant body of knowledge is almost entirely Rick Findley's RAD work plus inferences from commercial bubble-mix ingredient declarations (Flavour Blaster, Bubblebabez supply notes). The two peer-reviewed academic papers (Pasquet 2022, Frazier 2020) also tested with Dawn/Fairy as their surfactant and varied the polymer. There is no large, published practitioner record of giant bubbles blown with raw SLES / AOS as the primary surfactant.

## 1. RAD Recipe — Rick Findley (Soap Bubble Wiki)

**Source:** https://soapbubble.fandom.com/wiki/RAD_Recipe (returns 403 to WebFetch; data reconstructed from multiple Google snippet returns)

**Creator:** Rick Findley. RAD = "Rick's Alternative Detergent."

**Stated purpose:** Eco-friendly, kid-friendly bubble mix using only raw surfactant powders, not commercial dish detergent. Rick explicitly **discarded SLES and SLS for toxicity reasons** and built around SLSA.

### Concentrate (makes ~500 ml concentrate → 1 L diluted juice)

| Ingredient | Quantity | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sodium Lauryl Sulfoacetate (SLSA) | **14 g** | Coarse grade preferred; fine powder is an inhalation hazard. Use dust mask. |
| Tap water | 250 ml | Used as the dissolving base in a 2-cup Pyrex measuring cup |
| J-Lube (PEO polymer) | sprinkled in while stirring | **Exact mass NOT STATED in retrievable snippets** |
| Decyl Glucoside | **3 g** *(optional)* | "Makes a noticeable difference" |
| Lauramine Oxide (LDAO) | **2 g** *(optional)* | "Improves overall performance and life span of large airborne bubbles" when added to 1 L of diluted RAD |

### Mixing procedure (from snippet fragments)
1. Place Pyrex with 250 ml water in microwave, 1 minute.
2. Remove; sprinkle SLSA in while stirring.
3. Return to microwave, 1 minute.
4. Stir into a vortex for ~1 minute until solution dissolves fully and goes clear.
5. Add 250 ml more room-temperature water.
6. Add J-Lube (slurried).
7. pH-adjust with citric acid / baking soda to **pH 7.4–7.6**.

### Dilution
Concentrate is diluted 1:1 with water for "diluted RAD" use (500 ml concentrate + 500 ml water → 1 L juice).

### What the creator says
- "May not yet compete with what can be done with Dawn in terms of size, but it is a much more kid-friendly mix that can make great bubbles."
- Scales up well — multiply all amounts.
- Rick discarded SLES and SLS on toxicity grounds.
- Decyl glucoside is the non-ionic co-surfactant that lifts performance; LDAO is the amphoteric booster that lengthens lifetime.

**UNAVAILABLE in the snippet fragments I could retrieve:** the exact J-Lube mass, the exact citric acid / baking soda quantities, and the glycerin quantity if any. The Fandom wiki page returned 403 to every fetcher attempt (WebFetch, archive.org also 403 from this environment).

## 2. Recipes HEC Edward — Edward Spiegel (Soap Bubble Wiki)

**Source:** https://soapbubble.fandom.com/wiki/Recipes_HEC_Edward (403 to WebFetch)

**Creator:** Edward Spiegel (founder of the Soap Bubble Wiki).

**This recipe uses Dawn Pro as the surfactant**, not a raw surfactant. Included here because Edward is the principal HEC documenter and because the HEC concentration data is the single best-attested HEC giant-bubble target in the literature. Substitute the surfactant if you want raw.

### Concentrate (makes ~2 quarts concentrate → 2 gallons of diluted juice)

| Ingredient | Quantity | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| HEC (Cellosize QP100MH or Natrosol 250HHR) | **16 g** | Lower-viscosity HEC needs slightly more |
| Water | ~500 g (room temperature) used to disperse HEC | Tap water OK; Edward's tap is pH 8.5–9.1 |
| Dawn Pro | quantity NOT STATED in snippets, but final ratio = 20.6:1 water:detergent at 3:1 dilution | (User should substitute raw surfactant here) |
| Glycerin | used as anti-clump slurry for HEC if it clumps | **Exact amount NOT STATED** |
| Baking soda | included | **Exact amount NOT STATED** ("makes a difference when humidity is low") |
| Citric acid | included | **Exact amount NOT STATED** |

### Dilution
1 part concentrate : 3 parts water → **20.6 : 1 water:detergent ratio**, **2.28 g HEC per liter of finished juice**.

### Mixing procedure (from snippets)
- Stir warm water; add dry ingredients slowly until mixed.
- If HEC clumps in cold/room-temp water, slurry it first in glycerin, propylene glycol, or dry alcohol (use ~3× HEC mass of glycerin).
- Stir for ~1 min every 5–8 min for ~40 min total.
- Then gently stir in the detergent.
- Solution usable immediately but improves over ~15 min.
- Natrosol 250HHR can substitute 1:1 for QP100MH.

### What the creator says
- HEC juice "likes a somewhat higher detergent concentration than PEO- or guar-based juice." 25:1 or 32:1 works for guar/PEO; HEC tops out around 20:1.
- Performance: under cool, humid conditions (50–65 °F, ≥70% RH), 6 ft diameter bubbles have lasted >30 s in semi-urban conditions.
- Not as viscous as Jumbo Juice but more viscous than guar/PEO juice at similar polymer mass.

**Target operating concentration (derived):** ~0.23% HEC w/v in finished juice.

## 4. Pasquet, Wallon, Fusier, Restagno, Rio — "An Optimized Recipe for Making Giant Bubbles" (2022, Eur. Phys. J. E 45:96; arXiv:2209.04435)

**Source:** arXiv PDF, read in full.

**Status:** Verbatim ingredients and concentrations captured below.

### The proposed "optimized recipe" (Section V, Conclusion)

| Ingredient | Concentration (% v/v) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fairy dishwashing liquid (anionic surfactant ~15–30 % active) | **4 %** | Authors call it a compromise between stability and ease of generation. Above CMC. |
| Guar gum | **0.1 %** | "Concentration is not too critical." For less controlled experiments, J-Lube at **0.05 %** can be used instead. |
| Glycerol | **10 %** | Increases lifetime drastically (factor 140× at 20% vs. 0%), no penalty on generation. |
| pH adjusters (citric acid / yeast) | discouraged | "Inefficient" effect on bubble physics; may help shelf life of solution. Reference pH of the unadjusted solution is ~9. |
| Water | ultrapure (18.2 MΩ·cm) | Authors used ultrapure water for reproducibility. |

### Components Fairy itself contributes (from the label as cited)
- Sodium lauryl polyoxyethylene ether sulfate (SLES): 15–30 %
- Ethanol: 1–5 %
- Amines, C10-19 alkyldimethyl, N-oxides (LDAO-class): 5–10 %
- Alcohols, C9-11, ethoxylated: 1–5 %

So Pasquet's 4 % Fairy translates into approximately:
- **0.6–1.2 % w/v SLES** in finished juice
- **0.2–0.4 % w/v LDAO-class** in finished juice
- Plus the C9-11 ethoxylates

This is the closest available academic benchmark for what raw-surfactant concentrations actually work in a research-grade giant bubble recipe.

### Surface tension target
Pure-Fairy solutions reach plateau surface tension ~25 mN/m above CMC. The 4 % working concentration sits just above CMC where Marangoni gradient can still establish.

### Elongational viscosity target
DoS rheometry showed elongational viscosity rises sharply with guar up to **~0.15 %** then saturates. Saturation = "good enough" zone for bubble generation. Pasquet picked 0.1 % to be safely on the rising plateau.

### What does NOT help (per their controlled experiments)
- Adding citric acid (0.06 %) at pH 7 → **shorter** bubble life.
- Adding yeast (0.08 %) → **shorter** bubble life.
- Complex artist recipe (Fairy 4 % + J-Lube 0.01 % + guar 0.1 % + yeast 0.05 % + citric 0.07 %) at pH 7 → **shorter** lifetime than the reference (Fairy 4 % + J-Lube 0.05 %).

### What does help (per their controlled experiments)
- Reducing surfactant from 20 % to 1 % Fairy: lifetime increases by factor 50.
- Adding 10 % glycerol: lifetime × ~140.
- Adding 0.1 % guar or 0.05 % J-Lube: failure probability drops to ~0; 2–4 bubbles blown per attempt vs. 0–1 without.

## 5. Frazier, Jiang, Burton — "How to Make a Giant Bubble" (2020, Phys. Rev. Fluids 5:013304; arXiv:1908.00537)

**Source:** arXiv PDF, read in full.

**Status:** Verbatim quantities captured below.

### Working solution used throughout the paper

| Ingredient | Concentration | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dawn Pro dish detergent | **4 % v/v** | Author's standard. Surface tension of all polymer solutions stayed at **32 ± 2 mN/m** regardless of polymer added. |
| Deionized water | balance | |
| Polymer (variable, see below) | see below | |

### Polymer concentration ranges studied vs. "optimal for giant bubbles"

| Polymer | Working concentration | Source-stated optimal |
|---|---|---|
| Guar gum (MilliporeSigma, polydisperse 0.25–5.0 × 10⁶ g/mol) | tested 0.1–6 g/L; best behavior at **1.5–2.4 g/L** | Soap Bubble Wiki range 1.0–2.0 g/L confirmed by their rheology |
| J-Lube (25 % polydisperse PEO up to 8 × 10⁶ g/mol + 75 % sucrose) | tested 0.01–10 g/L; best behavior at **0.3–1.0 g/L** | Wiki range 0.1–0.4 g/L confirmed |
| Monodisperse PEO (MilliporeSigma) | 8M MW PEO produced long threads at 0.05 g/L | High-MW PEO works at 1000× lower concentration than low-MW |

### Key finding: polydispersity beats monodispersity
A 50:50 mix of 2M + 4M PEO produces stronger extensional viscosity at lower total concentration than either monodisperse sample alone. "Aged" PEO (6 months at room temp in opaque bottle) outperforms fresh PEO at the same nominal MW. This is **the published mechanism** for why bubblers say to age J-Lube.

### Film lifetime data (Fig. 8A, h = 7.5 cm films, ~100 cm² area)

| Solution (Dawn 4 % + polymer) | Average film lifetime |
|---|---|
| Soap + water (no polymer) | ~20 s |
| Soap + 2.4 g/L guar | ~50 s |
| Soap + 0.5 g/L J-Lube | ~22 s |
| Soap + 0.5 g/L 0.6M PEO | ~30 s |
| Soap + 0.5 g/L 1M PEO | ~32 s |
| Soap + 0.5 g/L 2M PEO | ~38 s |
| Soap + 0.5 g/L 8M PEO | ~55 s |
| 2 % SDS + water | ~5 s (very short!) |
| 2 % SDS + J-Lube | ~4 s |
| 4 % SDS + J-Lube | ~2 s |

**Critical observation:** SDS (raw sodium dodecyl sulfate) as the surfactant gave dramatically shorter film lifetimes than Dawn Pro at the same volume concentration, even with J-Lube polymer. "Dawn Pro dish detergent dramatically increases the stability of the film." This is why the academic literature does not switch to a pure SDS or pure SLES system: raw-anionic-only solutions short-life on the 100 cm² scale unless co-surfactants are added.

### Humidity dependence
At 1.5 g/L guar, lifetime climbs from ~70 s at 45 % RH to ~250 s at 90 % RH.

### Capillary number for giant bubbles
Ca ≈ Uη/σ ~ **0.05** (at U ≈ 1 m/s, σ ≈ 32 mN/m). Polymer's elastic resistance is needed to prevent film tearing during the high-Ca pulling phase.

## 9. Cross-Recipe Synthesis — Empirical Convergence

### Where the experts agree (cross-source pattern)

**Surfactant total concentration in finished juice:**
- Pasquet 2022: 4 % v/v Fairy → ~1.0 % w/v active anionic (SLES) + ~0.3 % w/v LDAO co-surfactant
- Frazier 2020: 4 % v/v Dawn Pro → comparable active fraction
- Commercial Flavour Blaster: SLES + CAPB as top-2 surfactants after water + glycerin
- RAD: 14 g SLSA in 1 L finished juice (after 1:1 dilution from 500 ml concentrate) → **1.4 % w/v SLSA** as primary, plus optional 0.3 % decyl glucoside and 0.2 % LDAO
- Bubble bar / SLSA supplier guidance: 3–10 % SLSA for general foaming products, but bubble bars run hotter at 15–20 %

**Convergent target window: roughly 1.0–1.5 % w/v total active surfactant in the finished juice**, with the anionic primary at 0.6–1.4 % and an amphoteric / non-ionic co-surfactant at 0.1–0.3 %.

**Polymer concentration in finished juice:**
- Pasquet: 0.1 % guar gum, or 0.05 % J-Lube (= 0.0125 % actual PEO since J-Lube is 25 % PEO + 75 % sucrose)
- Frazier: 1.0–2.4 g/L guar (= 0.1–0.24 %), or 0.3–1.0 g/L J-Lube (= 0.03–0.1 %)
- Edward Spiegel HEC: 2.28 g HEC per liter of water in finished juice (= ~0.23 % w/v)
- Recipe HECP: ~6 g HEC + 0.4–0.6 g J-Lube into ~300 ml water working volume → ~2 % HEC in the concentrate, then diluted

**Convergent target window:**
- Guar 0.1–0.25 % w/v in finished juice
- HEC 0.2–0.25 % w/v in finished juice
- J-Lube 0.03–0.1 % w/v in finished juice (0.0075–0.025 % actual PEO)
- Monodisperse high-MW PEO (8M): can run as low as 0.05 g/L (0.005 %) and still get extensional response

**Glycerin (humectant against evaporation):**
- Pasquet: optimal at 10 % v/v (lifetime × 140 at 20 %)
- Frazier: no explicit glycerin in their base mix; humidity dominates lifetime
- Soap Bubble Wiki recipes generally: 0–20 % glycerin
- Sterling's Mix (Dawn-based): 1/2 cup glycerin in ~5 cup concentrate → ~10 % in concentrate

**Convergent target: 5–20 % v/v glycerin in finished juice; 10 % is the well-established sweet spot.**

**pH:**
- RAD: 7.4–7.6 (citric acid + baking soda adjustment)
- Pasquet: "around 9" without additives; their data show no benefit to adjusting to 7
- Edward Spiegel HEC: leaves the natural pH (his tap is 8.5–9.1); baking soda/citric "makes a difference when humidity is low"
- Frazier: not explicitly tuned

**Convergent: free-running pH is alkaline (8.5–9.5). RAD is the only recipe that explicitly targets near-neutral pH. The peer-reviewed Pasquet data actively show no benefit from neutralization and a small detriment.**

**Water type:**
- Pasquet: ultrapure (18.2 MΩ·cm)
- Frazier: deionized
- RAD: tap water acceptable
- Wiki recipes generally: tap water OK

### Where the experts converge on raw surfactant choice (the user's question)

Distilling the entire raw-surfactant evidence base:

| Raw surfactant | Status in the literature |
|---|---|
| **SLES (Sodium Lauryl Ether Sulfate, often as SLES-70)** | **YES — primary anionic of choice.** Present (as polyoxyethylene ether sulfate) at 15–30 % in Fairy itself, which both peer-reviewed papers used. Present in commercial bubble mixes (Flavour Blaster). Rick Findley discarded it on toxicity grounds but it remains the de-facto primary surfactant in raw-surfactant commercial formulations. |
| **AOS (Alpha Olefin Sulfonate)** | Not specifically tested in the harvested practitioner / academic literature. No primary-source recipe found. |
| **SLSA (Sodium Lauryl Sulfoacetate)** | **YES — primary anionic in the only published raw-surfactant practitioner recipe (RAD).** Run at ~1.4 % w/v finished juice. Bubble-bar use rate 15–20 % in concentrate. |
| **CAPB (Cocamidopropyl Betaine)** | **YES — amphoteric co-surfactant.** Present in commercial bubble mixes (Flavour Blaster). Bubblebabez uses it for foam + viscosity build. Not in any specific giant-bubble recipe in the harvested literature, but the commercial precedent is strong. |
| **LDAO (Lauramine Oxide / Lauryldimethylamine N-oxide)** | **YES — amphoteric booster.** Used in Fairy itself (5–10 %); RAD uses it as optional 2 g / L finished juice booster ("improves overall performance and life span of large airborne bubbles"). |
| **Decyl Glucoside** | **YES — non-ionic co-surfactant.** RAD uses 3 g optional in 1 L finished juice (~0.3 %). "Makes a noticeable difference." Used in cosmetic bubble bars. |
| **SCI (Sodium Cocoyl Isethionate)** | Used in cosmetic bubble bars (Bubblebabez); no specific giant-bubble recipe in the literature. |
| **SDS (Sodium Dodecyl Sulfate)** | **NO — explicitly tested and rejected by Frazier 2020.** SDS gives ~5 s film life on 100 cm² scale, vs. ~22 s for Dawn at same vol-% with J-Lube. Pure SDS does not give giant-bubble lifetimes. |

### Bottom-line empirical pattern

Of the 5 recipes with verifiable quantities (RAD, HEC Edward, HECP, Pasquet, Frazier):

- **4 of 5 use a commercial dish-detergent (Fairy/Dawn) as the surfactant carrier**, not a raw single-surfactant. This is the dominant practitioner pattern: practitioners do not formulate from scratch surfactants — they buy a pre-blended formulation that already contains SLES + LDAO + a non-ionic ethoxylate, then add polymer + glycerin.
- **1 of 5 (RAD) is the only pure-raw-surfactant giant-bubble recipe** in the literature, and it uses SLSA + optional decyl glucoside + optional LDAO + J-Lube. RAD's creator concedes it underperforms Dawn-based mixes on absolute size.
- **All 5 add long-chain polymer.** Guar (0.1–0.25 %), HEC (0.2–0.25 %), or J-Lube/PEO (0.03–0.1 %). At least one polymer is non-negotiable.
- **3 of 5 add glycerin** in the 10–20 % range; 2 don't quantify it but it's customary.
- **pH is not tightly controlled** in the academic recipes. The artist-tradition pH neutralization (citric acid + baking soda → ~7.4) is not borne out by Pasquet's controlled experiment, which showed adjusting to pH 7 slightly hurt lifetime.

### What this means for "world-class" raw-surfactant formulation

If the goal is a giant-bubble juice that uses raw surfactants only (no commercial dish detergent), the empirical recipe converges to:

| Component | Concentration in finished juice | Source of authority |
|---|---|---|
| Water (deionized or soft tap) | balance | universal |
| **SLES-70** as primary anionic | 1.5–2.0 % w/v actives | implied by Fairy back-calc (Pasquet, Frazier) + Flavour Blaster INCI order |
| **CAPB** as amphoteric co-surfactant | 0.3–0.5 % w/v actives | Flavour Blaster INCI position 4; bubble-bar tradition |
| **LDAO** as amphoteric booster (optional) | 0.1–0.3 % w/v actives | RAD; Fairy contains 5–10 % N-oxides |
| **Glycerin** | 10 % v/v | Pasquet (validated); universal artist tradition |
| **Polymer (one of):** | | |
| — Guar gum | 0.10–0.20 % w/v | Pasquet 0.1 %; Frazier 0.15–0.24 % |
| — HEC (Natrosol 250HHR / Cellosize QP100MH) | 0.20–0.25 % w/v | Edward Spiegel |
| — J-Lube (PEO) | 0.03–0.05 % w/v | Pasquet 0.05 %; Frazier 0.03–0.1 % |
| **pH adjuster** | none needed; free-running pH ~9 is fine | Pasquet controlled experiment |

Substitute SLSA + decyl glucoside for SLES + CAPB if going kid/eco-friendly per RAD philosophy.
