# World-Class Giant Bubble Recipe v2 — Grounded Honest Rewrite

> **Premise:** Fairy works because of an *architecture* (SLES + CAPB + LDAO), not because of magic. Recreate that architecture from raw materials, drop the cosmetic junk (perfume, dye, preservatives, salt thickener, PEG-cocoate, citrate/NaOH pH adjusters), and add the two things Fairy doesn't have but giant bubbles need: **10 % glycerin** (Pasquet 140× lifetime) and **one polymer** at its documented working range. Everything in the mainline is grounded in primary-literature measurement or published practitioner recipes. The speculative bits from the previous v2 — Wind Tuning Matrix specific ratios, pH 7.4 buffer-lock, AOS pure-repulsion, NaCl+Aerosil+cetyl alcohol armor, multi-polymer 290 ppm scaffold — are demoted to Appendix A and clearly marked as untested hypotheses.
>
> **Headline numbers (per 1 L batch):** 875 g distilled water · 100 g glycerin USP (10 % v/v) · 20 g SLES-70 paste (1.4 % active) · 15 g CAPB-30 (0.45 % active) · 5 g LDAO-30 / Ammonyx LO (0.15 % active) · 0.5 g disodium EDTA · **polymer: pick ONE of three for a side-by-side bake-off — 1.5 g guar gum OR 2.2 g HEC Cellosize QP-100MH OR 0.4 g J-Lube**. **No pH adjustment** (lands free-running ~7.5–8.0; Pasquet measured neutralising hurts). Total surfactant actives **2.0 %**.

## 1. What this recipe is and why

**Goal:** longest-lived, biggest free-floating giant bubbles outdoors, grounded in measured science only.

**Primary evidence base:**

- **Frazier/Burton 2020** — measured raw SDS ≈ 5 s film life vs Dawn ≈ 22 s on 100 cm² film. Raw anionic alone underperforms commercial dish soap by 4×. The reason: dish soap is SLES + CAPB + LDAO *synergy*, not SLES alone.
- **Pasquet et al. 2022** — measured 140× lifetime increase at 10 % glycerin; measured 50× lifetime increase by *lowering* surfactant from 20 % to 1 % Fairy; measured that pH neutralisation to 7 *actively hurts* lifetime.
- **Acharya et al. 2020** — measured 3× extensional viscosity boost when surfactant adsorbs at CMC; CaBER thread time 3.12 s → 0.51 s with 1 % CaCl₂ (water hardness matters and must be chelated).
- **Mitrinova et al. 2013** — measured surface elastic modulus G' > 100 mN/m for SLES+CAPB+myristic systems; gives the mechanism for why amphoteric + non-ionic boosters help.
- **Flavour Blaster commercial INCI** — the only labelled SLES + CAPB + HEC bubble product on market; anchors the surfactant-to-polymer ratio.
- **RAD (Rick Findley)** — only documented pure-raw-surfactant giant-bubble recipe in the literature; its creator concedes it underperforms Dawn-based mixes. Confirms raw-surfactant work in this space is *under-explored*, not solved.

**The four ingredients doing the work in Fairy:** SLES + LDAO + CAPB + Water. Everything else in Fairy's INCI (perfume, dye, preservative, salt, PEG-cocoate, PPG-26, citrate, NaOH, dyes) is cosmetic, preservative, or actively counterproductive. We rebuild those four at matched concentrations and add **glycerin + polymer + EDTA**. That is the recipe.

## 5. Why each ingredient — primary literature grounded only

### 5.1 SLES + CAPB + LDAO at ratio 3.1 : 1 : 0.3

This ratio approximates Fairy Original's INCI ordering and the published Flavour Blaster commercial product (water / SLES / CAPB / HEC / glycerin / preservative). Mechanism:

- **SLES alone** gives only ~5 s film life on 100 cm² film (Frazier 2020 SDS proxy). An anionic surfactant lowers γ but doesn't build an elastic film.
- **CAPB co-adsorption** at the SLES interface increases packing density. The zwitterionic CAPB head sits between SLES heads, reducing electrostatic repulsion and tightening the monolayer. This is the synergy that takes dish soap from ~5 s to ~22 s.
- **LDAO** adds surface elasticity. Mitrinova 2013 measured G' > 100 mN/m for SLES+CAPB+myristic systems; LDAO substitutes for myristic without myristic's pH dependence (CMC drops with deprotonation pH ~6–7).

(MEASURED + EMPIRICAL — all three ingredients have controlled-experiment evidence in cited literature.)

### 5.2 Glycerin at 10 %, not 8 %

Pasquet 2022 measured the lifetime curve vs glycerin on a Fairy + guar base. Peak at **10 %** with **140× lifetime** vs 0 %. Above ~12 % bulk viscosity slows the wand. The previous v2 used 8 % targeting "pro-mix water-thin consistency" — that was a compromise, not the measured peak. 10 % is the measured peak. (MEASURED.)

### 5.3 No pH buffer — Pasquet's most counterintuitive finding

Pasquet 2022 ran a controlled experiment: titrated Fairy + glycerin + guar (free-running pH ~7.5) down to pH 7.0 with citric acid. **Lifetime dropped.** At pH 9 lifetime stayed roughly equal to free-running. Interpretation: CAPB's isoelectric point is ~6.25; at neutralised pH 7 CAPB sits close to isoelectric and aggregates with SLES, reducing free-monomer for fast interfacial adsorption. At free-running pH 7.5–8.0 CAPB is net-negative and stays in solution.

The previous v2's DKP buffer to pH 7.4 was based on theoretical reasoning about APAM dissociation. With APAM dropped from the mainline (Section 6 polymer bake-off doesn't include HPAM), there's no reason to buffer. (MEASURED contradicts the previous v2's theory.)

### 5.4 Disodium EDTA at 0.05 %

Disodium (not tetrasodium) keeps the mix from drifting alkaline. Log K = 10.7 with Ca²⁺ — ten orders of magnitude stronger than citrate (log K ≈ 3.5). At 0.5 g/L EDTA captures ~50 ppm Ca²⁺ equivalent — covers residual hardness in distilled water and gives defensive margin. (MEASURED, well-established cosmetic chemistry.)

### 5.5 No IPA, no salt, no preservative

- **IPA** was in the previous v2 to slurry dry PEO/PAM. With guar/HEC/J-Lube (all of which slurry directly into glycerin), IPA isn't needed. One fewer variable.
- **Salt** was in Fairy as a thickener. We get viscosity from polymer. (The NaCl-for-AOS-oscillatory-structural-force theory is in Appendix A2 because it depends on the AOS pure-repulsion mechanism, also speculative.)
- **Preservative** only matters for shelf life. Mix what you'll use in 2 weeks.

## 6. Polymer bake-off protocol — three candidates, side-by-side

We don't know which polymer wins on this base without testing. Three candidates, each at its documented working range:

| Candidate | Mass per 1 L | Active wt % | Source recipe | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Guar gum** | 1.5 g | 0.15 % | Pasquet 2022 measured optimum (scaled up from her 0.1 % on 4 % Fairy to 0.15 % on this lower-surfactant base) | Pasquet's measured optimum; cheap; food-grade | Calcium-sensitive (chelate hard); 12 hr hydration |
| **B. HEC Cellosize QP-100MH** | 2.2 g | 0.22 % | Flavour Blaster commercial INCI | Matches commercial product; calcium-tolerant; non-ionic robust | R-coating delays hydration; mid-MW only (~1.3 MDa) |
| **C. J-Lube** | 0.4 g | 0.04 % (0.1 g/L active PEO) | BLM / HECP practitioner | Polydisperse PEO out of the bag (~8 MDa tail; Frazier 2020 ✓); cheap | Sucrose carrier (75 % of mass) feeds microbes; shorter shelf life |

**Protocol:** mix three identical 1 L batches with everything *except* polymer. Then for each batch, slurry candidate A, B, or C into the glycerin and add to the surfactant solution. Rest 12 hours. Test all three side-by-side on the same day, same wand, same wind. Measure:

1. **Film lifetime** — Frazier-style 100 cm² horizontal hoop, average of 5 films, time to first hole.
2. **CaBER thread time** — thumb-pull a drop from a teaspoon, time until pinch-off. > 1 s is good; > 2 s is excellent.
3. **Single best bubble** — wand test, 10 dips per polymer, record largest survivor.
4. **Wind survival** — same wand, gusty conditions, count bubbles surviving > 5 s out of 20 launches.

The winner is the polymer for your conditions, water, and wand. *Don't pick blind.*

(EMPIRICAL — each polymer has individual published evidence; the side-by-side comparison on this exact base is unmeasured.)

## 7. Mixing protocol — no buffer, no acid, simple

### Step 0 — Pre-prep
1. Weigh ingredients on a 0.01 g precision scale (polymer dose is the only one that needs precision; surfactants tolerate ±5 %).
2. Sterilise a 2 L glass / HDPE bucket with boiling water + distilled rinse.

### Step 1 — Polymer slurry (T + 5 min)
In a 250 mL beaker: **100 g glycerin**. Sprinkle the polymer dose into the glycerin while stirring with a dry spatula. Glycerin pre-wets the polymer surface so it doesn't fish-eye when water hits it.

### Step 2 — Water + EDTA (T + 10 min)
In the 2 L bucket: **875 g distilled water + 0.5 g disodium EDTA**. Stir to dissolve. Optically clear.

### Step 3 — Polymer hydration (T + 15 min, plus 12 hr rest)
Slow paddle (NOT high-shear blender — that scissions UHMW polymer). Pour the glycerin/polymer slurry into the centre of the vortex in a thin stream over ~30 s. Continue gentle paddle for **5 min only**. Cover. **Rest undisturbed 12–24 hr** for full hydration.

### Step 4 — Surfactant integration (T + 24 hr)
Slow paddle the hydrated polymer solution. Add in order:
1. **20 g SLES-70** (over ~30 s)
2. **15 g CAPB-30** (over ~30 s)
3. **5 g LDAO-30** (over ~30 s)

SLES first (highest mass, sets baseline). CAPB and LDAO co-adsorb on top. Fold gently for 5 min — **don't whip up foam in the bucket**. Mix should be optically clear, very faintly straw.

### Step 5 — Rest and use (T + 24 hr → T + 72 hr)
Cover. Rest 24–48 hr cool dark. Performance peaks ~48–72 hr from initial polymer hydration. Decant 200–500 mL into a tray for sessions. Shelf life ~2 weeks unrefrigerated, ~6 weeks refrigerated.

**No pH check, no titration, no IPA, no salt, no buffer.** Deliberate.

## 13. Wind-tolerant variant — AOS + APAM + PEO at neutral pH

> **Status: SCIENCE-SUPPORTED ALTERNATIVE ARCHITECTURE.** Not the grounded SLES+CAPB+LDAO Fairy-recreation mainline (Sections 3–12), but a second-line build whose mechanism is well-documented in EOR and firefighting-foam literature and which the user's previous 816 g recipe partly demonstrated. Full citation trail in [[09-aos-science-deep-dive]].

### 13.1 Why this exists alongside the mainline

The Fairy-recreated mainline (SLES+CAPB+LDAO at free-running pH 7.5–8.0) optimises for **calm-air bubble life via dish-soap synergy** — the architecture that gives Fairy its ~22 s film life vs raw SDS's ~5 s (Frazier 2020 measured). It works well at free-running pH where CAPB is above its isoelectric point (pI = 6.25) and stays in solution as a synergy partner.

The user's previous best 816 g recipe (analysed in [[07-816g-recipe-analysis]]) used SLES+CAPB+LDAO+APAM+PEO at pH 6.1 — *below* CAPB's isoelectric point. That created a CAPB-SLES coacervate (ionic-crosslinked gel) which was extraordinarily strong in still air (100 ft up, 200 ft forward) but **shear-melts in any wind** — the same coacervate phase that gives glittering after 24 hours.

The wind-tolerant variant takes a different path: **pure-repulsion oscillatory disjoining pressure** film. AOS sulfonate + APAM (anionic polyacrylamide) at pH 7.5–8.0 — both anionic, both fully ionised, no coacervate. The film thins by **stratification** (polymer-mesh-fluid expelled one layer at a time) rather than catastrophic drainage; it resists turbulent shear by depletion-zone osmotic pressure rather than viscoelastic gel network. This is the same architecture used in enhanced-oil-recovery foams (Yekeen 2018; MDPI Energies 2023) and modern fluorine-free firefighting foams.

### 13.2 The recipe (1 L batch)

| # | Ingredient | g per 1 L | Active g/L | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Distilled water | ~875 | — | Carrier. Distilled mandatory for APAM. |
| 2 | AOS-40 (sodium C14-16 olefin sulfonate, 40 % active) | 60 | 24 | Primary anionic. **Better than SLES**: hard-water tolerant, pH 2–12 stable, no dioxane, mildly stretchier film. |
| 3 | LDAO-30 / Ammonyx LO | 5 | 1.5 | Optional non-ionic foam booster. Add at half mainline dose; primary stabiliser is the polymer mesh, not interfacial elasticity. |
| 4 | **CAPB-30** | **0** | **0** | **Deliberately omitted.** CAPB at pH below its pI 6.25 forms coacervate with anionics → strong in calm, melts in wind. Skip entirely. |
| 5 | Vegetable glycerin USP | 70 | — | 7 % v/v. Lower than mainline's 10 % because polymer mesh handles humectancy at this thickness. |
| 6 | APAM / HPAM 10–20 MDa (Flopaam 3330S or 3430S) | 0.15 | 0.15 | 150 ppm. The repulsion-network builder. Hydrolysed polyacrylamide is the documented best partner for AOS (Yekeen 2018, Bergeron 1996). |
| 7 | PEO 4–8 MDa (J-Lube or Polyox WSR-301) | 0.4 g J-Lube = 0.1 g PEO | 0.1 | 100 ppm PEO. Polydisperse backup polymer; Frazier 2020 measured polydispersity beats monodispersity. |
| 8 | Tetrasodium EDTA | 1.0 | — | 0.1 %. Critical for AOS+PEO — PEO degrades fast with trace metals; APAM aggregates with Ca²⁺. Slightly higher dose than the mainline because two polymer chemistries need protection. |
| 9 | **pH** | — | — | **7.5–8.0 free-running.** AOS+EDTA in distilled water lands here naturally. **No buffer.** |

**Total surfactant actives: 2.55 %** (24 g AOS + 1.5 g LDAO = 25.5 g/L). Slightly above the Pasquet ceiling — accepted trade-off for the pure-repulsion architecture which depends on AOS micelle density to set the oscillation period of the disjoining pressure.

### 13.3 Why each ingredient — citation trail

- **AOS over SLES**: hard-water tolerance (Linfield 1972 *JAOCS* lime-soap dispersancy; Yeserchem industry data), pH stability 2–12 (STPP Group; Pugh 2016), film stretchability (Farajzadeh 2008 NBF-vs-CBF transition data showing AOS sits longer in metastable CBF). MEASURED.
- **No CAPB**: CAPB pI 6.25 (Marinova 2009) is independent of anionic partner. AOS+CAPB at sub-isoelectric pH still forms coacervate. The science says raise pH or drop CAPB; we do both. SPECULATION-ELEVATED-TO-INFERENCE (no direct head-to-head measurement of AOS+CAPB wind-tolerance).
- **APAM at 150 ppm**: Bergeron 1996 *Langmuir* 12:5751 measured oscillatory disjoining pressure up to ~200 Pa from polyelectrolyte chain networks at 10 nm thickness; von Klitzing 2002 *J. Phys. Chem. B* 106:11769 measured polyelectrolyte mesh stratification step. Yekeen 2018 *J. Ind. Eng. Chem.* found AOS+HPAM gave most stable foam vs commercial baselines. MEASURED.
- **PEO redundancy**: Frazier 2020 polydispersity measurement. PEO at 100 ppm acts as a wider-relaxation-spectrum backup; aged-PEO/fresh-PEO blends fall within this architecture. MEASURED-mechanism + EMPIRICAL-blend-ratios.
- **pH 7.5–8.0**: APAM is fully ionised (carboxylates dissociate above pH 4.5 for the partially-hydrolysed grade); AOS is fully ionised; CAPB (if any trace remains) is above pI. MEASURED.
- **Glycerin at 7 %, not 10 %**: at high polymer mesh density the disjoining-pressure stabilisation dominates; extra glycerin viscosity slows wand uptake. EXTRAPOLATED.
- **EDTA at 0.1 %**: APAM aggregates with trace Ca²⁺ within hours; PEO chain-scissions in minutes with trace Cu/Fe. Tetrasodium chosen because mix already lands alkaline. MEASURED-mechanism.

### 13.4 Mixing protocol — wind-tolerant variant

The protocol differs from the mainline because polymer-first AND high-shear-avoidance are critical (APAM scissions catastrophically under high shear; once chains break the ends crosslink with trace metals).

1. **APAM slurry (T + 0):** in a 250 mL beaker, **70 g glycerin + 0.15 g APAM**. Stir with a dry spatula until polymer is wetted (it will not dissolve in glycerin alone — just disperse). Cover.
2. **J-Lube slurry (T + 5):** in a second 100 mL beaker, **20 g of the 875 g water + 0.4 g J-Lube**. Stir gently 30 s. Cover.
3. **Water + EDTA (T + 10):** in the 2 L bucket, **855 g distilled water + 1.0 g tetrasodium EDTA**. Stir until clear. Slow paddle.
4. **Add APAM/glycerin (T + 15):** pour the APAM-in-glycerin slurry into the centre of the vortex over 30 s. **Slow paddle only — NO blender.** High shear cuts APAM chains. Fold 5 min. Cover. **Rest 6 hr** for APAM hydration.
5. **Add J-Lube slurry (T + 6 hr):** slow paddle, pour J-Lube/water slurry into the centre over 30 s. Fold 5 min. Rest 6 hr.
6. **AOS + LDAO (T + 12 hr):** slow paddle. Add **60 g AOS-40** over 30 s. Then **5 g LDAO-30** over 30 s. Fold 5 min. Mixture should be optically clear, very pale straw, no foam in the bucket.
7. **Final rest (T + 12 hr → T + 60 hr):** cover, rest cool dark 48 hr. Performance peaks T + 48–72 hr.
8. **pH check (informational only):** should land 7.5–8.0. If below 7.0 add 0.05 g sodium bicarbonate per 1 L. If above 8.5 your distilled water was alkaline — switch source.

### 13.5 What this variant trades off vs the mainline

| Property | Fairy-recreation mainline | AOS+APAM+PEO wind-tolerant |
|---|---|---|
| Calm-air bubble size | Excellent (dish-soap synergy at high G') | **Best** (long extensional viscosity, stratification stability) |
| Wind tolerance | Good (no coacervate, but no rigid mesh either) | **Best** (pure repulsion, no shear-melt mode) |
| Single-bubble lifetime in still air | **Best** (Pasquet measured 140× at 10 % glycerin with surfactant synergy) | Good (lower glycerin, but polymer mesh extends lifetime) |
| Film stretchability | Good | **Best** (APAM 10-20 MDa extensional viscosity) |
| Shelf life | 2–6 weeks (PEO/J-Lube only if present) | 2 weeks (APAM aggregates with trace Ca²⁺ over time) |
| Cost per 1 L | ~£1.20 | ~£1.80 |
| Storage | Room temp OK | Room temp OK (no fridge) |
| Hard-water robustness | Good (EDTA-defended) | **Best** (AOS sulfonate vs SLES ether-sulfate) |
| Practitioner track record | Strong (Fairy + glycerin + polymer = decades of recipes) | Niche — pro commercial mixes only (Supapop, Bubblyheaven) |

**Bottom line:** the mainline is the safer, cheaper, longer-shelf-life starting point. The wind-tolerant variant is the science-supported answer to "the 816 g recipe was huge in calm but failed in wind — what swap addresses that?" Run both. The head-to-head answers the question.

### 13.6 Open questions for this variant

1. **Does 150 ppm APAM beat 100 ppm and 200 ppm?** Bergeron's oscillation depth scales as c^(−1/2); the working concentration depends on the wand fluid uptake. Untested.
2. **Does adding back trace LDAO at 0.15 % help, or is the polymer mesh already saturated?** Wagay 2015 measured AOS+LDAO CMC depression but no foam half-life. Untested for giant-bubble work.
3. **Aged-vs-fresh PEO ratio for this variant**: 50:50 is the practitioner-mainstream BLM/HECP guidance. Optimum for AOS+APAM is unmeasured.
4. **Does the mix degrade faster than the mainline?** APAM aggregates with trace Ca²⁺ in 1–2 weeks; the mainline's HEC tolerates Ca²⁺ better. Practical shelf life needs measurement.

### A1. Pure-Repulsion AOS+APAM at pH 7.4 *DKP-buffered* (former v2 mainline)

**This appendix entry covers the *DKP-buffered* version of the AOS+APAM architecture.** The general AOS+APAM+PEO architecture at **free-running** pH 7.5–8.0 has been promoted out of the appendix into Section 13 (wind-tolerant variant) following the literature review in [[09-aos-science-deep-dive]]. What remains here is the specific tightly-engineered version of the previous v2.

**Previous-v2 premise (SPECULATION):** AOS-40 anionic sulfonate at 2.4 % active with trace 8 ppm HPAM at pH 7.4 (DKP-buffered) works via a *pure-repulsion* mechanism — oscillatory disjoining pressure + steric stabilisation — without the SLES+CAPB+LDAO synergy. **Wind Tuning Matrix** with 22.5:1 PEO:PAM ratio, 70:30 aged:fresh PEO split for "gusty" conditions, 50:50 for "calm," 80:20 for "storm."

**Status of specific elements:**
- AOS+APAM mechanism: **VALIDATED** in deep-dive (Bergeron 1996, von Klitzing 2002, Yekeen 2018). Promoted to Section 13.
- DKP buffer to pH 7.4: **CONTRADICTED** by Pasquet 2022 measurement (neutralising hurts lifetime). Free-running pH 7.5–8.0 is the science-backed substitute. Section 13 drops the buffer.
- AOS 2.4 % concentration: **above** Pasquet's measured 1.5 % ceiling but justified by the pure-repulsion architecture's dependence on micelle density for oscillation period. Section 13 keeps this trade-off explicit.
- Wind Tuning Matrix specific ratios (22.5:1, 70:30 etc.): **EXTRAPOLATIONS**, not measured. Still speculative. Section 13 uses simpler 150 ppm APAM + 100 ppm PEO as a starting point with explicit "untested ratio" tagging.

**Why this DKP-buffered specific version remains in the appendix:** the buffer-locked pH 7.4 is specifically contradicted by measured science. The general AOS+APAM architecture without the buffer is now Section 13.
