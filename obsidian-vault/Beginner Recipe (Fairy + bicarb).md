# Beginner Recipe — Fairy + bicarb (UK supermarket only)

> **This is NOT the world-record recipe.** This is the easy/cheap variant you can build from any UK supermarket if you cannot get raw SLES/AOS/PAM. For wind tolerance and truly giant bubbles, use a proper SLES/AOS + PAM/PEO recipe at the correct pH (see your "AOS APAM Repulsion Network Physics" note in this vault).
>
> Specifically:
> - Fairy Liquid contains undisclosed cosurfactants and impurities that limit reproducibility.
> - Baking powder brings cornstarch and (often) aluminium phosphate with it. Pure sodium bicarbonate or dipotassium phosphate is cleaner.
> - This recipe holds up in still air. It tears in any wind above 3 mph.

## Ingredients (1 litre batch)

| Ingredient | Amount | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Tap water | 1000 g (1 L) | Base. Tap is fine unless your water is very hard. |
| Vegetable glycerin | 100 g (10%) | Slows evaporation. |
| Fairy Liquid (Original) | 40 g (4%) | The soap. Mixed-blend supermarket surfactant. |
| Isopropyl alcohol (90%+) | 15 g | Wets the J-Lube powder so it doesn't clump. |
| Baking powder | 2 g (½ tsp) | Buffers pH to ~7.4. (Cleaner: pure sodium bicarbonate.) |
| J-Lube powder | 0.5 g | The polymer (25% PEO + 75% sucrose). |

## Why this is a beginner recipe, not a world-record one

For wind tolerance the structure of the film matters more than ingredients. You want **oscillatory disjoining pressure** — a structural force inside the film that resists thinning under shear (a gust of wind). To get it you need:

1. **Anionic PAM at 100–200 ppm** (0.1–0.2 g/L), NOT at 50 ppm or below — below ~10 ppm PAM destabilises (bridging flocculation). Fairy doesn't include PAM at all.
2. **pH 7–9**, not 6.1, so PAM's carboxyl groups fully dissociate and the polymer extends into a rigid rod instead of collapsing into a coil. Baking powder pH 7.4 is close but inconsistent; sodium bicarbonate at pH 8.5 is the target.
3. **Clean anionic surfactant** (AOS-40 or raw SLES) above its CMC, so micelles crowd inside the film and stack into the oscillatory layers that supply the 200 Pa disjoining pressure.

Fairy + bicarb + 0.5 g J-Lube doesn't deliver any of those reliably. It will blow huge bubbles in dead-still air, then tear instantly when the wind picks up. That matches the user's reported experience.

## Mixing (for those who do want to make this beginner version)

1. Slurry: mix 0.5 g J-Lube + 15 g IPA + 100 g glycerin in a dry mug until the powder is fully wet.
2. Pour 1 L tap water into a clean bucket. Stir to make a small whirlpool, pour slurry into the centre, stir gently 2–3 min.
3. Sprinkle in 2 g baking powder, stir until dissolved (ignore the cornstarch sediment).
4. Pour in 40 g Fairy Liquid. Fold gently. No foam.
5. Cover, rest 24 hours. Peaks at 2–3 days.

## When to use this recipe

- Demoing to kids in a back garden on a still summer evening.
- You don't have raw chemicals on hand and need bubbles in the next hour.
- You want to compare against a known baseline before tuning the proper pure-repulsion or semi-IPN recipes.
